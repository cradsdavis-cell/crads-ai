## 1. What you are getting

Software, free of charge: the mineral image, the engine, the desktop app and
the documentation. You run it on a **mineral**: a server you create in your
own hosting account, holding an assistant and a brain that belong to you.
Either a **pebble** (one person's private workspace) or a **rock** (a
community's own mineral).

We maintain and publish the machinery: the container image, the engine, the
app, the scheduler and the system pages. You own the machine and the content:
your server, your brain, your pages, your skills, your cadence, your
connected services.

## 2. What it costs

**The software costs nothing, and that is not an introductory state.** There
is no plan to charge for it, no payment method collected, and no mechanism in
the software that could bill you.

What you do pay, to other people, on your own accounts: your hosting provider
for the server (billed by Hetzner to you), and Anthropic for your Claude
subscription. The setup wizard states both before anything is created.

Paid **services** exist separately: guided setup, hourly support, and
community onboarding. Each is agreed with you at a stated price before any
work happens. Buying none of them takes nothing away from the software.

We are not registered for GST, so no GST is charged.

## 3. Your accounts and your responsibility

- The hosting account, the server and every credential on it are yours. We
  hold no key to your mineral and cannot access, suspend or delete it.
- Your assistant runs on **your** Claude account under your agreement with
  Anthropic, and your usage is subject to it.
- **Scheduled skills spend your own Claude plan's usage.** Every scheduled run
  and every Telegram exchange is your assistant working on your account, and
  it draws on your plan's limits like any other use. The software floors
  repeating schedules at thirty-minute intervals for exactly this reason. If
  your plan runs dry, scheduled runs fail until it recovers; that is your
  plan doing what plans do, not a fault.
- You are responsible for what you ask your assistant to do and for what you
  connect it to. Automating a decision is still your decision, as the

- Anything you put on your mineral must be lawful and yours to put there.

## 4. What we promise

- **We do not read your notes, and we could not.** Nothing of yours routes
  through us: no account, no directory, no telemetry. The
  [privacy policy](/docs/privacy-policy) sets out the one exception honestly:
  a support session you deliberately grant.
- **We tell you when something is broken**, including when we caused it.
- **Every fix ships with the test that pins it.** That is how this is built
  and it is the reason to trust a fix rather than a reassurance.
- **You can leave with everything**, trivially, because everything is already
  yours: your brain is a git repository you hold, on machines you hold.
- **We give notice.** If we stop publishing the software or wind the project
  down, you hear it with a date attached. What a wind-down means is set out
  in the [continuity page](/docs/continuity) and those commitments are part
  of these terms.

## 5. What we do not promise

Being specific is more use to you than a disclaimer.

- **No uptime guarantee, because uptime is not ours to guarantee.** Your
  mineral runs on your server at your hosting provider. What we promise is
  visibility: the app shows each skill's last run and its outcome, so a
  missed run shows up as a missed run rather than as silence.
- **No guarantee your assistant is right.** It is a language model working
  from your notes. It will sometimes be confidently wrong. Check anything
  that matters before you act on it, and never treat its output as legal,
  financial, medical or tax advice.
- **No guarantee a connected service keeps working.** Google, Microsoft and
  the rest change their APIs and their rules whenever they like. When one
  breaks we fix it if we can and tell you if we cannot.
- **No guarantee of data recovery beyond your own backup.** A pebble takes a
  nightly encrypted backup to a repository you own; a rock is backed up when
  its owner chooses to. If no repository is connected and the machine dies,
  the content that only existed there is gone. Connect the repository.
- **We are one person.** Not a company with a support rota. Response times
  depend on one human being awake and not on a mountain.

## 6. What the software may do to your mineral

- **Update it.** A pebble restarts nightly onto the latest published
  software; a rock updates when its owner presses Update. Either way the
  change is machinery, not your files, and you can switch the nightly update
  off. Old software keeps old bugs.
- **Let a helper in, only when you grant it.** Support access needs a grant
  from you, expires on a clock the mineral's own door enforces, and the grant
  is recorded on your own mineral (what is and is not logged is set out in
  the [privacy policy](/docs/privacy-policy)). There is no standing key.

There is no suspension power and no kill switch. We could not act against
your mineral if we wanted to, which is the design.

## 7. Ending it

**You, at any time.** Delete the server in your own hosting console and the
arrangement is over. Nothing needs our involvement, no reason is required,
and nothing is clawed back: your repository, your backups and your files stay
yours.

**Us, with notice.** If we stop publishing software or providing services, we
say so with a date, per the [continuity page](/docs/continuity). Your mineral
keeps running regardless.
