## 1. What a community host is, legally

You are a **curator and referrer**. Not a reseller, not a data processor, not
our agent, and, since September 2026, not anyone's hosting provider.

Concretely: **each of your members runs their own mineral, on their own
hosting account, under their own agreement with us** (the
[terms](/docs/terms)). You introduce them, you vouch for the product, and you
curate the shared library their minerals pull. What you do not do is stand
between them and their own machines or data, because the machinery for that
does not exist.

This matters more than it sounds. It means:

- Your members' privacy is not yours to manage, because you never hold their
  data.
- If a member has a problem with the software, it is the project's to answer,
  not yours.
- If you stop hosting the community, your members' minerals do not notice.

## 2. What you cannot see, and why it is not a promise

You **cannot read** your members' brains, notes, mail, messages or calendars,
and you receive no status feed about their machines at all. This is not a
policy we are asking anyone to trust. The commons is a repository their
minerals pull, read-only, one way; nothing of theirs is written back up, and
you hold no credential for any member's box.

What you do see: your own commons repository, your own roster of grants, and
whatever your members choose to tell you themselves.

## 3. What you are responsible for

- **Who you let in.** Membership is read access you grant on your git host.
  If someone should not be in your community, that is your call and your
  call alone.
- **What you publish.** The skills, pages, prompts and folders in your
  commons are your content, under your name. Keep them accurate, and run
  what you publish before you publish it. The software helps in one narrow
  way: publishing refuses material that contains anything secret-shaped,
  naming the file and line, so a refusal at publish time is protection
  working rather than a fault.
- **Backing up your rock's own brain.** Your rock does not back itself up
  nightly; its backup is a deliberate act by you, from the app, and a rock
  that has never been backed up dies with its machine. The
  [backup page](/docs/back-up-and-restore) walks it.
- **Not overselling the product.** Do not promise your members capabilities
  the product does not have. If you are unsure whether something is true,
  ask before you say it.
- **Your own fees, stated plainly.** If you charge members for onboarding,
  curation or membership, that is your arrangement with them, on your
  invoice. Say clearly that the software itself is free and that their
  hosting is billed to them by their hosting provider, so nobody discovers a
  bill they thought was yours.

## 4. What we are responsible for

- The machinery: the image, the engine, the app, the scheduler, the updates.
- Answering members' problems with the software.
- Telling you before we change anything that changes what you have promised
  them.
- **Not going around you.** We will not market to your members on the back of
  your introduction, and we will not use your community's name as a
  reference without asking you first.

## 5. Your name and your intellectual property

Your community's name, brand and material stay yours. We use them only where
you have said we can, and hosting a community is not permission to put you on
a website.

**Anything you or your members put in a commons or on a mineral is not ours
and we will not use it.** Your playbook, published to your community, is
between you and them.

## 6. Money

We charge you nothing to host a community: no licence fee, no per-member fee,
no setup fee, and there is no machinery through which such a fee could
arrive. Paid services (guided setup for your members, onboarding help,
support hours) are available and priced per engagement, in writing, before
work happens.

We are not registered for GST, so no GST is charged.

## 7. Ending it

**You, at any time and without a reason.** Stop publishing, or archive the
commons repository. Your members keep their minerals, and copies of anything
they installed stay theirs: withdrawing content stops new installs and
removes nothing anyone holds.

**Us, with notice**, per the [continuity page](/docs/continuity): if we stop
publishing the software, your community's commons keeps working, because it
is a git repository between you and your members.

Either way, no member loses anything because the arrangement between you and
us ended. That is the whole reason everything sits where it does.

## 8. What we cannot promise you

You are putting your name to this in front of people who trust you, so you
should have the risks in writing rather than in a conversation.

- **We are one person.** No support rota, no runway, no board.
- **The software will have bugs**, and your members will find some of them.
  Every fix ships with a test that pins it, which is the honest version of a
  guarantee.
- **If we wind down**, the commitments in the
  [continuity page](/docs/continuity) apply, and the biggest one is
  structural: nothing your members depend on runs through us.
