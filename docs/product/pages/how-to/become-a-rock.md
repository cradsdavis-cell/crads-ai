A rock is a community's own mineral: the hub that curates what the community
shares. If you run a community, a cohort or a team, starting one is two
distinct moves: a mineral wearing the rock face, and standing up the
[commons](/docs/rocks-and-pebbles) your members will pull from. Nobody
approves any of it; there is nothing central to ask.

What a rock is **not**: a machine that hosts, creates, or can reach into
anyone else's machine. Your members make their own pebbles with the same
wizard you used, on their own accounts. What you are taking on is curation
and care, not custody; the [hosting agreement](/docs/hosting-a-rock) is the
written version of that split.

## The one thing to understand first

One thing changes hands in the upgrade, and it is the big one: **your
personal brain becomes the rock's brain.** The pages your assistant knows you
by become the pages the community runs on, and what your rock later publishes
draws on them.

That is the right shape for a founder whose working brain *is* the
community's brain. If yours is full of things that are yours alone, the
honest move is to keep this mineral as your own and set up a second mineral
for the community instead, with the same wizard, on the same account.

When promotion runs, it takes this seriously enough that it will not run
until you have typed that exact fact back at it, and a verified copy of your
brain is pushed to your own repository first, so your pre-rock self is
always recoverable.

## Where promotion stands today

**Turning a pebble into a rock is temporarily not offered in the app.** The
old promote flow was built around the hosted platform's machinery, which the
self-host pivot removed, and the commons-era version of the upgrade is still
being built. The **Your rock** card on Your pebble says exactly this.

What that means in practice:

- **If your mineral is already a rock**, nothing here affects you: both
  faces keep working, and everything below applies as written.
- **If you are starting a community fresh**, you do not have to wait for
  promotion. The community itself is a
  [commons](/docs/share-with-your-community): a git repository you own.
  What the rock face adds is the Catalogue page's publishing and roster
  tooling around that repository, so for now a new community needs a mineral
  that is already a rock, or a host willing to curate the repository by hand
  until the upgrade returns.
- Your brain's safety story is unchanged either way:
  [back up first](/docs/back-up-and-restore), always.

## The first thing to do as a rock

Not inviting. **Your rock's own [interview](/docs/the-interview)**: the same
eight layers, asked about the community instead of about a person. Everything
you later publish to members inherits its coherence from these answers, so do
it before you curate anything.

## Then stand up the commons

The community itself is a git repository you own, and
[share with your community](/docs/share-with-your-community) walks the whole
of it: create the repository, point the Catalogue page's Commons card at it,
publish your library into it, and hand each member a join bundle. Membership
is read access to that repository, granted and revoked with the git host's
own tools; [manage your members](/docs/manage-members) covers the roster
side.

## Your maintenance changes today

A rock does not look after itself the way a pebble does. The nightly backup
and the nightly software update are pebble habits; on a rock both become your
deliberate acts. [Running your rock](/docs/run-your-rock) is the whole of
that job, and none of it is daily.

## What starting a community does not give you

It does not let you see into your members' minerals, and under the commons
model it does not even give you a status feed about them. Their machines are
theirs, on their own accounts, holding only their own keys. What you can see
(nothing) and why that is the design is
[what your community can see](/docs/what-your-community-can-see); the craft
of hosting people well without surveillance is
[hosting a community well](/docs/host-a-community-well).
