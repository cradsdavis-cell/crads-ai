*Only what waits on your answer.*

A rock's queue. It exists because a host running a community accumulates
things that nobody else can settle, and scattering them across five pages is
how one gets missed for a fortnight.

## What it is now

Smaller than it used to be, and honestly so. Under the
[commons model](/docs/rocks-and-pebbles) membership is a grant on your own
roster plus read access on your git host, so there is no central queue of
join requests, anchor asks or device approvals feeding this page any more:
those flows retired with the hosted platform in September 2026.

What still genuinely waits on a host lives in two places:

- **This page**, for anything the rock's own machinery stages for your
  answer.
- **Your git host**, for share-backs: a member proposing content to your
  commons arrives as a pull request there, not here, and reviewing it is the
  git host's ordinary review flow
  ([share with your community](/docs/share-with-your-community)).

## Why it is deliberately narrow

The page shows what is *waiting*, not what has happened. History belongs on
the thing it happened to; a queue that also carried a log would stop being a
queue.

If this page is empty, nothing is waiting on you here. That is the whole
design: an empty Decisions page should mean you can close the app, and it can
only mean that if nothing else quietly needs you elsewhere. The weekly look
that catches the rest is on [running your rock](/docs/run-your-rock).
