There is a lot of noise around AI assistants, and most of it describes a chat
window. This is not that, so it is worth saying carefully what it is.

An AI executive assistant is an assistant that **lives in your working life
rather than in a tab**. It holds a real, readable model of your world (your
goals, your people, your commitments, your voice) and it acts on a schedule as
well as on request. The difference is not intelligence. It is context and
residence, and [how your assistant thinks](/docs/how-your-assistant-thinks)
is the one model that makes the rest of these pages make sense.

## The test that separates it from a chatbot

Ask a chat window "what should I focus on today?" and you get advice-shaped
filler, because it does not know you. It cannot: you arrived through a login
page thirty seconds ago.

Ask an assistant that holds your goals, reads your calendar and your inbox, and
knows which three commitments are slipping, and the same question has an actual
answer. That is the entire category difference in one question. Everything else
follows from it.

## What changes about a week, concretely

Not magic. A handful of small, compounding removals:

- You stop opening your inbox to *decide* things. Triage happened at seven,
  before you were up.
- The day starts with three priorities picked against your actual goals, not
  eleven calendar entries: the daily brief.
- The threads where somebody owes you a reply stop silently dying: the
  follow-up sweep.
- The week gets planned on Monday and reviewed on Sunday, against the same
  written outcomes.

Each is minor. Together they are the difference between running your week and
your week running you, and none of them requires you to remember to ask. Each
arrives as a recipe in the library on your mineral, with its own guide beside
it; the [Skills page](/docs/skills-page) is where they land.

## The skills it ships with

The four cadences above are the daily shape; the everyday set is short enough
to name in full: the **Daily brief**, **Inbox triage**, **Follow-ups**,
**Plan your week**, the **Weekly review**, **Capture the session**,
**Connect a service**, **Build pages and cards**, **Explain this system**,
and the **Onboarding interview**. The engine also ships **Write a page**,
which builds you a page of your own. That is the whole built-in set:
anything beyond it is something you added, and the
generated table at [every skill your mineral ships with](/docs/skills) is
the authoritative list, straight from the code, of what each one does.

To take one apart: a follow-up sweep surfaces overdue tasks, unanswered email
threads from the people who matter to you, chat messages in both directions
(anything inbound and unreplied for over a day, anything you sent that has sat
cold for five), project next-actions that have gone stale, people left in
limbo, and exactly one recommended next step. One, not three. A list of next
steps is a way of not choosing.

## The week is a closed loop

Plan your week sets three outcome goals on a Monday (five at most) and writes
them to a durable page in your brain. The daily brief reads that page every
morning and weighs the day's top three against it, so "what should I focus on
today" is answered against what you said the week was for. The weekly review
closes the loop at the other end: it reads the same week, says what moved and
what did not, and proposes next week's focus, which is exactly the seed the
following Monday's plan picks up.

Set, read every morning, reviewed, reseeded. Without the setter, nothing else
has anything to check against, which is why it exists.

## Nothing runs, sends, or labels without you

Every skill ships switched off; a schedule exists only because you flipped it
on. Inbox triage proposes its buckets and asks for one bulk confirm before a
single label is written, and the threads where a reply is owed are never
touched at all. Scheduled skills that produce outbound messages draft them;
none sends on its own.

The same honesty applies to content. "If a source returns nothing, say so,
never fabricate" is a literal rule written into the briefing skills, so a
morning brief with only two real priorities says two rather than padding to
three. And proactive runs respect your quiet hours: an interval schedule
carries a pause-overnight option, on by default.

## Your world, in files you can open

The model it holds of your world is not an embedding or a proprietary store.
It is ordinary files: linked, readable pages on your own machine, versioned in
git. The structure they arrive in is a starting template, not a schema; you
can restructure your brain freely and nothing in the machinery assumes a
particular layout.

That is also why the assistant can explain itself. Ask it "what am I connected
to" or "where is my data" and the Explain skill inspects the live mineral,
read-only, and answers from what is actually there rather than reciting a
brochure. If it cannot determine something, it says so.

## Who this is actually for

**A business owner** is the centre of the target. One person holding customers,
suppliers, pricing, a pipeline and a family in their head, with no assistant
because assistants cost a salary. The interview moves that world out of their
head into pages an assistant can act on: [how](/docs/the-interview).

**A founder** is the same shape earlier: more moving parts than hours,
relationships that die of silence, and a calendar that eats the strategy time.

**A coach or a consultant** is the same shape with other people's worlds in
it: clients, sessions, promises made in a room and forgotten by Thursday. The
assistant holds the history so the person can hold the relationship.

## What it is not

**Not a virtual assistant.** A VA brings judgement and hands; this brings
memory and cadence. A VA can negotiate with your landlord. This can make sure
you never again forget that the negotiation is due, draft the email in your
voice, and hold the history. Plenty of people need both.

**Not an automation platform.** Tools like n8n and Zapier move data between
apps along rails you build, and they are good at it. This is the other end:
you do not build rails, you have a conversation, and the judgement calls stay
visible. When something *should* be a hard rail, a scheduled skill is one, but
the product does not make you think in flowcharts.

**Not a chatbot with your logo.** The empty version of this product is exactly
that, which is why [the interview](/docs/the-interview) is treated as the
product and not as onboarding friction.

## The honest prerequisites

It needs an hour of genuine interview before it is any good. It needs a small
hosting bill of your own, because it runs on a server in your own account and
nobody hosts it for you. It needs [connections](/docs/connections-page) to
your real accounts, with the [Google one](/docs/connect-google) costing ten
minutes because your key stays in your own hands. And it repays attention: an
assistant you correct gets sharper, one you only consume from plateaus, and
[grow the brain](/docs/grow-the-brain) is how.

If that trade sounds right, [your first hour](/docs/first-hour) is where it
starts. And if you came with one specific question, the [FAQ](/docs/faq)
answers the usual ones straight.
