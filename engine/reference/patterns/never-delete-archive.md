title: Never delete, archive (move aside with a reason, don't destroy)
maturity: assistant-can-adopt-now
---

## What it is

Durable or shared state (the brain, any record connected to a real business relationship) is never destructively deleted. It's moved aside to a dated archive location with a stated reason, so nothing is unrecoverable by accident. Actual deletion needs a specific, per-item human go-ahead, and is reserved for genuinely disposable working material, never anything describing the business, its customers, or its history.

## Why it matters

A business owner's brain accumulates context whose value isn't obvious in the moment it's created. A busy owner saying "get rid of anything old" is much more safely interpreted as "move it out of the way and show me a list" than as "delete it right now": the cost of a wrong delete (lost customer history, a supplier note nobody can reconstruct) is far higher than the cost of an extra archive folder sitting unused.

## How to adopt it on this box

- Cheap, trivial-effort convention: any time content would be deleted, move it to a dated archive location instead and name the reason for the move.
- Only actually delete after the client gives explicit per-item confirmation on a shown list. Never interpret a blanket "clean it up" as license to delete without a list first.
- Apply this to brain pages and any connected external record (a leads sheet, a shared document) alike. The "move, don't destroy" default is the same regardless of where the content lives.
- The one carve-out: genuinely disposable, fully reproducible working files (a scratch render, a superseded draft with no standalone value) can be hard-deleted without the archive step. That's a narrower exception, not the default.

## Signals you need it

- Anyone has ever had to ask "did you delete that?" and not been sure of the answer.
- Something the client wanted "cleared out" turns out to have been load-bearing after the fact.
- A cleanup instruction was interpreted more aggressively than the client meant it.
