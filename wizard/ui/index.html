Rock setup

Set up your rock's hub

The hub is the control centre that creates and looks after each of your members' AI assistants. Everything runs on accounts you own, and your passwords never leave this computer except to go straight to the services you signed up for.

What am I actually setting up?
Two things get built. Your hub (one control centre for your whole rock) and, later, one private assistant for each member. You run everything from the hub: you add members, and you decide what content and updates flow down to them. Members never see each other, and you never see inside a member's private assistant. You can take the whole thing down whenever you like.

Step 1 of 7 · Your rock

1 · Your rock

Just the basics. You can change any of this later.

What goes here?
Lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens only, no spaces (for example acme-collab). It shows up in web addresses, so keep it short.
Who should this be?
Whoever should have full control of the hub. Separate more than one with commas.
Where does this show up?
Everywhere a person reads it: your own panel, your members' panels, and the name your rock's assistant answers to. The short handle above stays the web address.
What's this for?
The web address your rock owns (for example yourorg.com). Each member gets their own sub-address under it, like alex.yourorg.com.

2 · Member features

Every assistant comes with the essentials (a second brain, daily and weekly check-ins, email and calendar, and a dashboard). These two are optional extras. Whatever you choose here is the same for everyone, so your whole rock stays on one product.

Telegram messaging + scheduled check-ins (each member turns it on themselves)
Voice assistant (the member connects their own voice accounts)
Why can't I change this?
The assistant never emails or messages anyone on its own. It writes the draft; a human reviews and sends. This can't be turned off, it's one of the core safety guarantees every rock ships with.

3 · Your vocabulary

The features are fixed, but the words are yours. Your members will see these labels everywhere, so use the names your rock already uses. You can change them anytime later.

What's a content area?
The buckets your program content falls into. A coaching rock might call them Areas; a college might call them Subjects.

4 · Connect your accounts

Crads-AI runs on three services that you own and control. You'll create a free account with each and paste one access code from each below. About 15 minutes the first time.

New to this? Open “Show me how” under each service for click-by-click steps. Already have your access codes? Just paste them in.
Why three accounts, and is my money safe?
Hetzner runs the computers, Cloudflare gives your web addresses secure connections, and GitHub privately stores your rock's brain. You own all three. Crads-AI just borrows an access code from each so it can set things up for you. Nothing is billed to us, you stay in full control, and you can cancel any code at any time.
HetznerRuns the computers · about €9/month per member, billed to you by Hetzner
Show me how to get this code
  1. Go to console.hetzner.cloud and sign up (free to start).
  2. Create a new project, call it anything.
  3. In the left menu open Security, then the API tokens tab.
  4. Click Generate API token, choose Read & Write, and name it (e.g. crads-ai).
  5. Copy the code it shows you and paste it below.
Open Hetzner ↗
CloudflareGives your web addresses secure connections · free
Show me how to get this code
  1. Go to dash.cloudflare.com and sign up (free), then add your domain if it isn't there yet.
  2. Top-right, open My Profile → API Tokens → Create Token.
  3. Choose Create Custom Token (not the “Global API Key”).
  4. Add these four permissions exactly: Account · Cloudflare Tunnel · Edit; Account · Access: Apps and Policies · Edit; Zone · DNS · Edit; Zone · Zone · Read.
  5. Under Zone Resources pick your domain, create the token, and copy it (it's only shown once).
Open Cloudflare ↗
GitHubPrivately stores your rock's brain · free
Show me how to get this code
  1. Go to github.com and sign up (free) if you don't have an account.
  2. Open Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens → Fine-grained tokens.
  3. Click Generate new token. Under Repository access choose All repositories.
  4. Set these permissions: Administration · Read and write; Contents · Read and write; Metadata · Read. Nothing else.
  5. Generate it, then copy the code and paste it below.

5 · Your rules

How your rock's assistants behave. Three rules are locked on: they're safety guarantees every rock gets, and the system won't run without them.

Privacy & what flows between you and members
🔒The assistant never sends on its own · it drafts, a human always sends.Always on
🔒You can't see inside a member's assistant · ever. The two signals below are the only things a member can choose to send up to you.Always on
Weekly pulse: a short update a member can choose to share with you
What's the pulse?
A brief weekly summary a member opts into sharing, rolled up onto your hub dashboard. They choose to send it; you never see inside their assistant.
Health signals: tiny “is it working” pings from each member's assistant
Is any content shared?
No content, just health, like a heartbeat. Turn this off and you won't know when a member's assistant has a problem you could help with.
Direct drops: let you push a one-off note or file to a single member
How does this work?
Sends a one-off message or file straight into one member's inbox. Handy for something that isn't part of your regular content.
Honesty about AI: assistants say they're AI when a person would assume otherwise
Who can do what
What can an admin do?
Full control of the hub. Leave blank to use the admin emails you gave in step 1.
What can support do?
Help members day-to-day, but they can't delete anything, change these rules, or touch secrets.
App access: members connect the assistant app with their own secure key
Browser access: a zero-setup way in stays available too
When someone joins or leaves
🔒A member's assistant and data are theirs · if they leave, nothing is deleted; your content updates just stop.Always on
Your content updates reach members automatically (packs, templates)
Only upgrade the underlying software when you choose to (never silently)
What happens to their data?
Their assistant and everything in it always stay theirs. This is only about when your program content stops updating for them.
Which should I pick?
Choose whichever is closest to most of your members, for the fastest experience.
