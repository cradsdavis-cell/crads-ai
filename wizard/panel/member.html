Software update: restart your assistant's mineral

        
If you were told an update is out, or your assistant seems stuck, this restarts its mineral and picks up the software the Crads-AI release channel has published for it. It takes about a minute; everything of yours (your brain, your files, your settings) stays exactly as it is.

If something on your mineral needs fixing, you can let Crads AI (the software maker) in for a limited time. There is no standing access: nothing opens until you grant it, it expires on its own, you can revoke it at any moment, and every grant is logged right here.

Google Workspace, with your own key

          
        

        
You'll create your own private key for your Google account. It stays yours: Crads-AI never sees your Google password, and the key works nowhere but this mineral. You sign in once, and you choose exactly what it can reach: email, calendar, files, each has its own tick-box on Google's screen, and you can change your mind later by signing in again.

          Step 1: Create your keyFive short console visits, each one button away
          

            
Each button opens the exact Google page. Do the one thing it asks, tick Done, move on. About five minutes in all.

            

              
1. Create a project. Name it anything you like, then press Create.

              

                Create a project
                
              

            

            

              
2. Turn on the services. One confirm turns on Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Tasks and Contacts for your key.

              

                Turn on the services
                
              

            

            

              
3. Name the app. Give it any name and use your own email for both contact fields.

              

                Name the app
                
              

            

            

              
4. Say who can use it. Choose External, then press Publish app so its status says In Production. Google may mention that public apps need verification: that applies to apps other people use, and this one has exactly one user, you. Publishing is what makes your sign-in permanent instead of weekly.

              

                Say who can use it

          Step 3: Sign in with GoogleTwo clicks, one screen that looks scarier than it is
          

            
Google will say it hasn't verified this app. True: the app is yours, you created it minutes ago. Click Advanced, then Go to your app's name. Google then lists each thing your assistant could reach (Gmail, Calendar, Drive and so on) with a tick-box beside it: tick what you want to share and leave out anything you don't. You can sign in again any time to add or remove services.
